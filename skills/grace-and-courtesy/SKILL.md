# Practice one social situation

Prepare language and a calm rehearsal for a situation that already happens. Courtesy is local to the family's culture.

## When to use

A concrete social pinch keeps returning: grabbing a toy, interrupting, hugging without asking, joining a game, greeting a relative.

If someone is being hurt right now, use `respectful-limits`. If the issue is a whole routine, use `montessori-family-routines`.

## Inputs

- The recurring situation in one observed example
- Who is present (sibling, visiting child, adult)
- Home language and the child's communication mode
- Cultural greeting and touch norms the family actually uses
- What the adult already modeled
- Whether anyone was harmed (if yes, repair is part of the plan)

## Procedure

1. Describe the situation without motive: "Took the truck from a visitor's hands."
2. Choose one skill for this week: asking for a turn, asking for help, saying no to a hug, waiting to join.
3. At a calm time, the adult models the words or signs slowly. Hapa Family, "Grace and Courtesy," uses everyday modeling and brief role-play at neutral times; asking before hugs and accepting refusal; adult apologies; avoiding forced apologies. Keep those. Do not keep any eye-contact rule.
4. Offer the child's real mode: speech, sign, gesture, picture, or device. A point at "turn" on a board counts.
5. Invite optional role-play with toys or with a willing adult. Stop when the child leaves.
6. If harm already happened, the adult helps repair: restore the object, check the other person, and offer a real apology from the adult when the adult contributed. Do not script a child apology as the price of returning to play.
7. Check the environment: is there a duplicate toy, a clear place to wait, a way to join without grabbing?

Montessori, *Own Handbook*, "Freedom," welcomes shared joy and help. It does not define respect as a posture, a gaze, or a spoken formula.

## Output

- Situation (factual)
- Adult model (exact words/signs)
- Child options, including a non-speech form
- Optional role-play steps
- Repair steps if harm occurred
- Environment tweak
- Observation: did the child have a usable way to ask or refuse?

## Adaptations

Match greetings to the family's culture. For 6-12, practice group-entry language and online/offline peer requests the family actually faces. For adolescents, practice consent and repair with more privacy and fewer puppet shows.

## Safeguards

Do not require eye contact, speech, touch, or a hug as proof of respect. Do not force an apology. Modeling supports practice; it does not guarantee later behavior.
