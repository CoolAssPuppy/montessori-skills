# Evidence: grace-and-courtesy

## Original Montessori

- Maria Montessori, *Dr. Montessori's Own Handbook*, 1914, "Freedom," pp. 77-80. Calm guidance, help, companionship; shared joy is allowed. [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/29635).

## Modern professional guidance

- NAEYC equity recommendations: value home languages and cultures; include disabilities and family structures. [Recommendations for ECE](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/position-statements/equity/recommendations-ECE).
- AMS [Montessori for Children with Disabilities and Neurodivergences](https://amshq.org/about-us/montessori-children-with-disabilities-and-neurodivergences/). Accommodations and partnership; not evidence that Montessori treats a diagnosis.

## Hapa Family (practitioner / parent)

- [Grace and Courtesy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jNu5ybZy398). Help requests; turns; asking before hugs and accepting refusal; adult apologies; avoid forced apologies. Discard the video's eye-contact expectation as a rule.

## Editorial adaptations

- AAC/sign/gesture as first-class participation is an editorial access requirement.
- "No forced apology" follows Hapa and current safeguarding sense; it is not a quoted Montessori statute.
- Claims that modeling guarantees later behavior are stronger than the evidence in H3.
