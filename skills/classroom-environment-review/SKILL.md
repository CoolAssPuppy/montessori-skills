# Audit the classroom environment

Look at how people actually move and work. Fix hazards before aesthetics.

## When to use

A guide or coordinator reviews the room after observation, a complaint, or a term start.

Home spaces: `prepared-home-environment`. Individual materials: `material-care-and-rotation`. Work-period staffing: `mixed-age-work-cycle`.

## Inputs

- Observation notes from real use
- Age band / program
- Floor plan or a walk-through list
- Known access needs
- School safety rules for anchoring, chemicals, and kitchen tools

## Procedure

Walk paths first, then shelves.

1. Movement: aisles, carrying routes, line of sight, wheelchair turning space, clutter at floor level.
2. Workspaces: enough tables or rugs for the attendance you actually have; light; noise.
3. Materials: complete, in sequence the album uses, reachable without climbing. Control of error only works if the piece exists (*Method* ch. XII, XX).
4. Order: a child can restore a work. Storage that only adults can decode is a barrier.
5. Hazards: unanchored shelves, frayed cords, water near outlets, missing non-slip, unlabeled chemicals. Adult presence does not prevent a tip-over ([CPSC Anchor It!](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/Video/Anchor-It-How-to-Anchor-It-and-protect-children-from-furniture-tip-overs)).
6. Access: communication supports in view, alternative seating, captions or visual schedules the school already uses. Preserve needed accommodations even when they look "busy."
7. Propose a short list. Each change names the barrier, owner, and a date to watch use. *Spontaneous Activity* ch. V: proportion, real work, usable apparatus. AMS prepared-environment language is program guidance. Do not score beauty. Do not claim sensorial materials treat vision, hearing, or sensory conditions (*Method* ch. XIV is historical physiology).

Immediate hazards at the top.

## Adaptations

Review at the height and speed of the children who use the room, including seated users.

## Safeguards

Historical breakables need a modern risk assessment. Attractive furniture still needs anchoring.
