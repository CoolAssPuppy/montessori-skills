# Observe a prospective school

Walk in with the family's needs written down. Separate what you saw from what the school said.

## When to use

A family will tour, has just toured, or is comparing programs.

After enrollment, switch to `montessori-school-transitions`. For decoding a term used on the tour, use `montessori-terms-explained`.

## Inputs

- Child's age band and the program under review
- Family priorities (hours, language, access, outdoor time, siblings, cost)
- Known access needs
- Any status the school claimed (member, verified, candidate, accredited)
- What the family already read on the website

This packet has not visited or endorsed any school.

## Procedure

1. Write the family's must-haves and nice-to-haves before the door opens.
2. On the visit, watch without interrupting work. Note mixed-age grouping, whether children choose work, how adults speak to children, and whether materials look complete and in use. AMS lists trained teachers, mixed-age classrooms, Montessori materials, child-directed work, and uninterrupted work periods as core components ([AMS core components](https://amshq.org/the-ams-difference/core-components-of-montessori/)). Those are AMS program markers, not a scoring rubric you invented.
3. Use AMS's parent checklist as questions, not as a grade: safe/clean premises, age-level teacher credentials, meaningful engagement, respectful interaction, parent information ([The AMS Difference](https://amshq.org/the-ams-difference/)).
4. Ask what the school can actually provide for this child's access needs ([AMS disability page](https://amshq.org/about-us/montessori-children-with-disabilities-and-neurodivergences/)). A hopeful answer still needs a named service, hours, and specialist.
5. If the school states AMS membership, verification, candidacy, or accreditation, write which word they used. Those four stages differ. Verify against an authoritative current record before repeating the claim.
6. After the visit, sort every note into **observed**, **school-claimed**, or **unanswered**. A quiet, attractive room does not establish quality or fit.

## Output

- Family priorities
- Visit checklist (questions + watch-fors)
- Factual notes
- Three-column comparison: observed / claimed / unanswered
- Follow-up questions for the school
- Status-verification step if a credential was mentioned

## Adaptations

Ask whether tours can be seated, captioned, translated, or done at a quieter time. Bring the child's access questions in writing.

## Safeguards

Do not photograph children. Do not request other families' private records. Do not treat this skill as an accreditation inspection.
