# Evidence: respectful-limits

## Original Montessori

- Maria Montessori, *The Montessori Method*, 1912, ch. V: active discipline, community limits, rejection of prizes; also historical isolation of children who disturbed others. Use the limit principle. Do not operationalize isolation. [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/39863).
- Maria Montessori, *Dr. Montessori's Own Handbook*, 1914, "Freedom" and "Moral Factors": available adult; work related to liberty. [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/29635).

## Modern professional guidance

- NAEYC equity recommendations on dignity, family structures, and observer bias. [ECE recommendations](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/position-statements/equity/recommendations-ECE).
- CDC [Act Early](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/families/index.html) for persistent developmental or safety concerns.

## Hapa Family (practitioner / parent)

- [Managing Transitions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CeZ-e-0zxKI) and [Grace and Courtesy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jNu5ybZy398): calm practice later; no forced apologies. Do not generalize physical follow-through into restraint.

## Editorial adaptations

- Immediate safety, then a one-sentence limit, then an environmental review: this skill's sequence.
- Historical isolation and coercive examples are rejected as procedure.
- Quiet/eye contact as non-evidence of learning is an editorial safeguard.
