# Respond without humiliation

Stop harm. Keep the child a member of the family. Look at the setting once everyone is safe.

## When to use

Someone was hit, something dangerous was thrown, a child ran toward a street, or work was destroyed. Need the actions as seen, who was at risk, age band, known supports, and what the adult already did.

Later language practice: `grace-and-courtesy`. Repeating daily jam: `montessori-family-routines`.

## Procedure

1. **Describe the event as actions**, e.g. threw the puzzle toward Sam's head. Do not invent a motive.
2. **Protect bodies and exits.** Move people apart. Remove the object. Block the street. Call emergency services if needed. Use the least handling that stops the danger. This skill does not teach restraint holds.
3. **State the limit in one short sentence** in the home language or as a visual/device message: "I will not let you hit." Then offer a safe alternative: space, a different object, an adult body between siblings, a drink, a quiet corner the child may leave. For older children, name the effect and restore what can be restored; for adolescents, add privacy and a later conversation.
4. **Stay present.** Montessori, *Own Handbook* "Freedom" and "Moral Factors," keeps the adult available and connects liberty to possible work. Historical isolation of children who disturbed others in *The Montessori Method* ch. V is descriptive history. Do not prescribe seclusion.
5. **After calm, inspect conditions:** sleep, hunger, sensory load, unclear turn-taking, adult rush, inaccessible communication. Change one of those. Practice the social script later with `grace-and-courtesy`, not as a lecture during distress.
6. **Record what you saw:** time if known, action, what stopped the harm, what you will try next. If the same serious pattern keeps returning, talk with a qualified clinician or the school's designated safeguarding person. Do not diagnose from classroom observation ([CDC Act Early](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/families/index.html)).

Quietness and eye contact do not prove the child has learned the limit.

## Output

- Neutral event description
- Immediate safety actions taken
- Adult script (one limit + one alternative)
- Environment or routine change
- Follow-up observation question
- Referral note if the family already has a concern, without a label you invented

## Safeguards

No humiliation, threats, withheld meals, forced affection, or isolation. No spoiled-food consequence. No "you must look at me." If a weapon, a head injury, or abuse is in play, stop skill work and get qualified help.
