# Prepare a conference agenda

Turn what the family has seen into a short list the guide can answer.

## When to use

A conference is scheduled, or the family wants a structured meeting.

Guides writing the school's report should use `progress-report-drafting`. For a single confusing word, use `montessori-terms-explained`. For the message that books the meeting, a guide uses `family-school-messages`.

## Inputs

- Family's own examples (dates if known)
- School reports, work, or notes the family was given
- Questions they already have
- Access, language, or friendship concerns
- Any screening or specialist process already underway (do not start one here)

## Procedure

1. List strengths as observed facts: work chosen, help given, something the child said or signed.
2. List concerns the same way. Separate "what we saw" from "what we fear it means."
3. Compare family notes with the school's supplied report. Mark matches and gaps. Do not assign motives to the guide.
4. Choose at most five questions. Useful topics: engagement, access, friendships, next lessons, how home can help without copying the classroom. Elise Arevalo (AMI) asks for objective notes and family partnership; referral when concerns persist ([See, Support, Share](https://montessori-ami.org/trainingvoices/observation-and-intervention)).
5. Put developmental worries on the agenda in plain words. CDC Act Early: talk with the child's doctor when milestones are missed or the family is worried. The conference does not replace that.
6. Leave space for the teacher's evidence and for agreed next actions. Do not prewrite a contract.
7. Heather White (AMS, 2023): listen, ask clarifying questions, share goals. Put one family goal on the page.

A6 (Essential Elements, late 1990s public-school guidance) names conferences, observation, and portfolios as assessment tools. Use the school's actual conference format.

## Output

One page:

- Strengths (factual)
- Family examples
- Questions
- Priorities (ranked)
- Support or access items
- Proposed follow-up (who, by when) left blank for the meeting
- Missing evidence

## Adaptations

Offer the agenda in the home language. Allow a support person. If the child attends, agree which parts are for adults only.

## Safeguards

Do not diagnose. Do not invent reassuring anecdotes. Do not name other children. Do not treat the draft as an agreement.
