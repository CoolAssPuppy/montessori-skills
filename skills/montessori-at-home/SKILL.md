# Start Montessori at home

Help a family choose one manageable next step from the home and child in front of them.

## When to use

The family wants to begin or restart Montessori at home with a real room, child, and limited time. Need age band, one interest the adult saw, known access needs, tools already owned, adult time this week, and any swallowing or medical instructions already in force.

Hazard layout: `prepared-home-environment`. One named task: `everyday-independence`.

## Procedure

1. Restate the observed interest as an action, e.g. stood at the sink and poured from a cup.
2. Match age to the kind of work, without a deadline. Under 3: one care or movement task with close supervision. 3-6: a complete everyday task. 6-12: a project or reason-seeking follow-up (*To Educate the Human Potential*, ch. I-II). Adolescent: a shared household or community job the family actually holds (AMI excerpts from *From Childhood to Adolescence*, pp. 65-75).
3. Choose **one** accessible change from what they own: low hook, liftable pitcher, shoe basket, stable stool. Hapa Family "5 Lessons Learned After 5 Years of Montessori Parenting" prefers existing tools; still screen small parts, cords, and climbable furniture.
4. Name **one** real activity: wipe a spill, prepare a safe snack, water a plant, pack a bag. Demonstrate the hard movement slowly, invite in the home language (speech, sign, gesture, or AAC), stay available. The child may refuse.
5. Write one review question for 3-7 days later: what the child did, what help was needed, what got in the way.
6. Stop. Do not add a second activity, a shopping list, or a classroom schedule.

*The Montessori Method* ch. V-VII treats independence as taught with patience and usable tools, not a school shelf at home. Do not copy chapter VII's sample timetable as a home clock.

If the family raises a missed milestone or a worry that will not wait, point them to their clinician and [CDC Act Early](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/families/index.html). "Follow the child" does not delay that conversation.

## Output

- Observed interest (family's words, factual)
- One accessible change, cost, and who prepares it
- One purposeful activity, materials already owned, supervision note
- Review question and a date the family chose
- Assumptions labeled if a detail was missing
- Seated or raised surfaces and a clear turning radius when needed

## Safeguards

No branded shopping list. No choking-size food or objects for a child who mouths. No floor mattress described as an approved infant sleep product. No spoiled food left out as a lesson. Adult help stays available.
