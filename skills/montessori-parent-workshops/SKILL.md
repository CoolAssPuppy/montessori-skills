# Plan one parent workshop

Start from a real family question. Teach one thing they can try.

## When to use

Families asked about a topic (cleanup, observation, limits) or the school runs parent education.

Invitation: `school-announcements`. Survey themes: `family-feedback-review`. Home task detail: `everyday-independence`.

## Inputs

- The question families actually asked
- Age band of the children
- Time available
- School's practice (what this classroom really does)
- Room access, language, childcare if offered
- Sources you will cite

## Procedure

1. One objective: "By the end, a parent can demonstrate a two-step cleanup invitation." CDC QTS §§1-2: need and objective. This is adapted training design, not a CDC-branded course.
2. A6 Recruitment/Parent Education: explain principles and curriculum the school uses. White: shared expectations.
3. Separate **Montessori primary**, **practitioner example**, and **present safety adaptation** in the outline. Example: Hapa cleanup video may inform limited choices; spoiled food as a consequence stays out.
4. Demonstration must be safe and affordable (objects the family likely has). Invite practice. Check understanding without ranking parents.
5. Handout: steps, one source, one limit. Feedback and a follow-up resource (QTS §§6-8).
6. Scheduling, recording, and publication need approval.

Do not lecture theory without a try-it. Do not diagnose children from audience stories.

## Output

- Audience need
- Objective
- Timed outline
- Demonstration and practice
- Handout bullets
- Source list with locators
- Feedback questions
- Follow-up
- Access notes

## Adaptations

Interpreters, captions, seated practice, large-print handout. Evening and daytime options if the school offers both.

## Safeguards

No product sales pitch. No guaranteed outcomes. Do not present the outline as externally reviewed Montessori training.
