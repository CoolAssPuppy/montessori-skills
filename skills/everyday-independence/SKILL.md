# Teach one everyday task

Turn one ordinary job into a visible sequence the child can try, with help that can shrink or return.

## When to use

The family named a single task: putting on a jacket, washing hands, slicing a banana, feeding a pet, packing a lunch, wiping a table.

If the problem is the whole morning, use `montessori-family-routines`. If the tools are out of reach or unsafe, fix the space with `prepared-home-environment` before teaching.

## Inputs

- The exact task and why it matters this week
- Age band and known motor, communication, or energy limits
- Hazards: heat, blades, choking-size food, animals, outdoor streets, chemicals
- What the child already does unprompted
- How much time a weekday attempt actually has
- Whether a previous plan failed (what happened, not a label for the child)

## Procedure

1. **Name the task and the hard part.** "Jacket: finding the second sleeve" is a plan. "Be more independent" is not.
2. **List prerequisites you can see:** object within reach, safe water temperature, seated work for food, an adult free to stay. Missing prerequisite becomes an adult preparation step, not a child failure.
3. **Analyze the movements.** Borrow Montessori's dressing-frame habit from *Own Handbook*, "Motor Education": isolate the difficult motion, show it slowly, use few words. Do not invent a school album sequence.
4. **Demonstrate once or twice facing the same way the child will work.** Pause. Invite. Accept refusal.
5. **Help from least to more support:** wait; point; offer a spoken, signed, or AAC cue; stabilize the object; do the hard sub-step and leave the rest. Return help on a hard day. Hapa Family, "Guiding Cooperation with Routines + Cleaning Up," treats a previously mastered task as still allowed to need help.
6. **Let repetition belong to the child.** Stopping is allowed. Do not restart the task as a test.
7. **Write one observation prompt** for the next attempt: starting condition, what the child did, what the adult did, whether the child continued after a pause.

Age notes:

- Under 3: one short cycle with the adult's hands ready. Food texture follows current choking guidance.
- 3-6: a complete cycle with cleanup of that task, adult nearby.
- 6-12: a household job with a real result (Hapa, older-children video; treat 6-12 breadth as interpretation).
- Adolescent: a responsibility the family actually shares, with safety remaining adult-owned where the law or risk requires it.

Montessori, *The Montessori Method* ch. V-VI, asks for patient teaching and a concise presentation when the child is attending. It does not set an age deadline for jackets or knives.

## Output

- Task analysis (steps, hard step, hazards)
- Adult preparation
- Demonstration notes (few words, visible hands)
- Invitation language in the home language, plus a non-speech option
- Assistance ladder
- Observation prompt
- What not to do on a rushed morning (adult completes the task without a lecture)

## Adaptations

Swap buttons for a larger fastener if that is what the family owns. Offer a seated dressing option. Use a visual sequence only if this child uses it. Keep hygiene available even when the child declines to "practice."

## Safeguards

Do not shame, withhold food, or use spoiled leftovers as the next lesson. Do not hand over hot pans, medicines, or street crossing. Do not treat a missed attempt as a developmental score.
