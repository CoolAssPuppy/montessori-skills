# Teach one everyday task

Turn one ordinary job into a visible sequence the child can try, with help that can shrink or return.

## When to use

The family named a single task (jacket, hands, banana, pet, lunch, table). Need age band, known limits, hazards, what the child already does, and weekday time.

If the problem is the whole morning, use `montessori-family-routines`. If tools are out of reach or unsafe, use `prepared-home-environment` first.

## Procedure

1. **Name the task and the hard part**, e.g. jacket: finding the second sleeve.
2. **List visible prerequisites:** object within reach, safe water temperature, seated food work, an adult free to stay. Missing pieces become adult preparation.
3. **Analyze the movements.** Borrow Montessori's dressing-frame habit from *Own Handbook*, "Motor Education": isolate the difficult motion, show it slowly, use few words. Do not invent a school album sequence.
4. **Demonstrate once or twice facing the same way the child will work.** Pause. Invite. Accept refusal.
5. **Help from least to more support:** wait; point; offer a spoken, signed, or AAC cue; stabilize the object; do the hard sub-step and leave the rest. Return help on a hard day. Hapa Family, "Guiding Cooperation with Routines + Cleaning Up," treats a previously mastered task as still allowed to need help.
6. **Let repetition belong to the child.** Stopping is allowed. Do not restart the task as a test.
7. **Write one observation prompt** for the next attempt: starting condition, what the child did, what the adult did, whether the child continued after a pause.

Age notes: under 3, one short cycle with hands ready and current choking textures; 3-6, a complete cycle plus cleanup, adult nearby; 6-12, a household job with a real result (Hapa older-children video; breadth is interpretation); adolescent, a shared responsibility with safety still adult-owned where law or risk requires it. *The Montessori Method* ch. V-VI asks for patient teaching when the child is attending. It sets no jacket or knife deadline.

## Output

- Task analysis (steps, hard step, hazards)
- Adult preparation
- Demonstration notes (few words, visible hands)
- Invitation in the home language, plus a non-speech option
- Assistance ladder
- Observation prompt
- Rushed-morning fallback: adult completes the task without a lecture
- Seated dressing, larger fasteners, or a visual sequence if this child already uses them

## Safeguards

Do not shame, withhold food, or use spoiled leftovers as the next lesson. Do not hand over hot pans, medicines, or street crossing. Do not treat a missed attempt as a developmental score.
