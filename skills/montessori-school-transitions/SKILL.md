# Plan a school or classroom change

Build a plan that uses the receiving school's actual arrival, comfort-object, and pickup rules.

## When to use

A child is starting school, moving rooms, changing programs, or returning after absence. Need written arrival/goodbye/comfort-object/medication/pickup rules, named adults at the door, home language, and health or sensory notes already shared through approved channels.

Choosing a school: `montessori-school-visit`. Later reports: `montessori-conference-preparation`.

## Procedure

1. Read the school's current notes. If a policy is missing, list it as unanswered. Tours and S7 (Tours and Dennis, NAEYC *Young Children*, Sep 2015) support collaboration, a familiar object if allowed, an arrival buddy, and visual or auditory cues. Peer support must not replace adult duty of care.
2. Rehearse an optional goodbye sequence at home: who walks in, what is said or signed, who holds the bag, where the adult stands. Hapa Family, "Managing Transitions," favors a predictable sequence over a rigid clock. Skip hugs and eye contact if the child does not want them.
3. Identify the designated staff member at the door and the verified pickup person. Write how the family will hear about the day (the school's actual channel).
4. Prepare one allowed familiar object and, if the school agrees, a family photo or a short recording in the home language. Check choking, magnets, and privacy before sending objects or images.
5. Agree when adults will review. Share observations as facts ("stood at the window for 10 minutes"). Write the plan in the family's language; include mobility routes, a quiet wait option, and a charged AAC device staff know how to use.
6. For older children, replace preschool props with maps, locker practice, timetable walk-throughs, and a named adult. Keep home prep, arrival, named support, pickup or independent departure, review. Do not assign a peer as the responsible adult.

Heather White's AMS communication article (20 Sep 2023) asks families and staff to share goals from the start. Put one family goal and one school goal on the plan. Do not invent a fixed-day settling-in period.

## Output

- Home preparation steps
- Arrival sequence
- Designated staff
- Pickup / departure
- Allowed comfort supports
- Communication channel
- Review conversation (when, who, questions)
- Unanswered policy items

## Safeguards

Do not force separation against an agreed support plan. Do not photograph other children. School messages remain drafts until the family or school sends them.
