# Review a home area

Inspect one real space so a child can take part without turning the room into a classroom display.

## When to use

The family names a room or routine that is hard to use: kitchen, bathroom, entry, bedroom, outdoor step. They want the child to reach, carry, and put things away.

If they only want a first activity, use `montessori-at-home`. If they want the teaching steps for one task, use `everyday-independence`.

## Inputs

- Room and the routine that happens there
- Who uses the space, including younger siblings
- Age band and known mobility, sensory, or communication needs
- Furniture that can tip, climb, or block a path
- Where chemicals, knives, heat, medicines, batteries, and magnets live
- Infant sleep arrangements, if any
- Budget and whether they rent (what they may not drill)

## Procedure

Walk the space in the order a child would use it.

1. **Path.** Can the child enter, turn, and carry something to a table without climbing? Note cords, rugs that slide, and blocked routes for a walker, crutches, or a wheelchair.
2. **Reach.** What does the child actually use every day: cup, toothbrush, coat, snack? Place a few of those within a safe reach. Keep detergents, medicines, knives, matches, batteries, and magnets under adult control.
3. **Stability.** Anchor climbable shelves, dressers, and televisions with hardware that fits the wall and the lease. Adult presence does not replace an anti-tip device ([CPSC Anchor It!](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/Video/Anchor-It-How-to-Anchor-It-and-protect-children-from-furniture-tip-overs)).
4. **Parts and mouthing.** Check whole objects and broken pieces. U.S. small-parts rules cover many products intended for under-3s; exemptions are not a safety certificate ([CPSC small parts FAQs](https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/Small-Parts-and-Choking-Hazard-Labeling-FAQs)).
5. **Sleep, if this is a bedroom for an infant.** Current CDC/AAP-based guidance: supine sleep, firm flat safety-approved surface, no soft bedding, room sharing ([CDC infant sleep](https://www.cdc.gov/sudden-infant-death/sleep-safely/index.html), 17 Sep 2024). Do not call a mattress on the floor an approved infant sleep product. Do not recommend hammocks or seating naps.
6. **Food work, if this is a kitchen.** Seated upright eating, texture and size suited to the child, continuous adult supervision ([CDC choking hazards](https://www.cdc.gov/infant-toddler-nutrition/foods-and-drinks/choking-hazards.html), 11 Mar 2026).
7. Pick **three** changes at most, ordered as: immediate hazard, then one access change, then one optional comfort change. Assign an adult owner for each.

Montessori, *Spontaneous Activity in Education*, ch. V, asks for furniture proportioned to the child's reach and strength, and for work that is real. *Own Handbook*, "Motor Education," uses everyday care tools. Historical breakable crockery is not an injury goal. Do not score the room for beauty.

## Output

A prioritized table:

| Area | Hazard or barrier | Change | Cost / already owned | Adult responsibility | Check after use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Add a short list of items that stay adult-controlled.

## Adaptations

Describe reach from the child's actual position (seated, standing with support, floor). Keep a communication board or typical signs where the child looks during that routine. If drilling is forbidden, name tension straps or relocating climbable pieces away from the child's path, and say the limit.

## Safeguards

Do not give children free access to cleaners, hot tools, or medicines. Do not leave spoiled food as a cleanup consequence. Do not treat mouthing interest as permission to use small parts.
