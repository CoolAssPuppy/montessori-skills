# Review a home area

Inspect one real space so a child can take part without turning the room into a classroom display.

## When to use

The family names a hard-to-use room (kitchen, bathroom, entry, bedroom, outdoor step). Need who uses it, age band, mobility or communication needs, tippable furniture, where chemicals/knives/heat/medicines live, infant sleep if any, budget, and whether they may drill.

First activity: `montessori-at-home`. Teaching steps: `everyday-independence`.

## Procedure

Walk the space in the order a child would use it.

1. **Path.** Can the child enter, turn, and carry something without climbing? Note cords, sliding rugs, and blocked routes for a walker, crutches, or wheelchair. Describe reach from the child's actual position (seated, standing with support, floor).
2. **Reach.** Place a few everyday objects (cup, toothbrush, coat, snack) within a safe reach. Keep detergents, medicines, knives, matches, batteries, and magnets under adult control.
3. **Stability.** Anchor climbable shelves, dressers, and televisions with hardware that fits the wall and the lease. Adult presence does not replace an anti-tip device ([CPSC Anchor It!](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/Video/Anchor-It-How-to-Anchor-It-and-protect-children-from-furniture-tip-overs)). If drilling is forbidden, use tension straps or move climbable pieces, and say the limit.
4. **Parts and mouthing.** Check whole objects and broken pieces. U.S. small-parts rules cover many products for under-3s; an exemption is not a safety certificate ([CPSC small parts FAQs](https://www.cpsc.gov/FAQ/Small-Parts-and-Choking-Hazard-Labeling-FAQs)).
5. **Sleep, if this is a bedroom for an infant.** Current CDC/AAP-based guidance: supine sleep, firm flat safety-approved surface, no soft bedding, room sharing ([CDC infant sleep](https://www.cdc.gov/sudden-infant-death/sleep-safely/index.html), 17 Sep 2024). Do not call a mattress on the floor an approved infant sleep product. Do not recommend hammocks or seating naps.
6. **Food work, if this is a kitchen.** Seated upright eating, texture and size suited to the child, continuous adult supervision ([CDC choking hazards](https://www.cdc.gov/infant-toddler-nutrition/foods-and-drinks/choking-hazards.html), 11 Mar 2026).
7. Pick **three** changes at most, ordered as: immediate hazard, then one access change, then one optional comfort change. Assign an adult owner for each.

*Spontaneous Activity in Education* ch. V asks for furniture the child can use; *Own Handbook* "Motor Education" uses everyday care tools. Historical breakable crockery is not an injury goal. Keep signs or a communication board where the child looks during that routine.

## Output

A prioritized table:

| Area | Hazard or barrier | Change | Cost / already owned | Adult responsibility | Check after use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Add a short list of items that stay adult-controlled.

## Safeguards

Do not give children free access to cleaners, hot tools, or medicines. Do not leave spoiled food as a cleanup consequence. Do not treat mouthing interest as permission to use small parts.
